# Recovering an interrupted plan move

A website restart keeps unfinished plan moves held. In **Plans → Recent plan moves**, the signed-in owner can choose **Adopt existing destination** when source cleanup was durably verified. The operation checks the recorded destination account and harness, current employee binding/running intent, stopped source and live destination composer. It changes only the move journal: no session is started, stopped or rebound, and the old handoff is not replayed.

Source cleanup proof is recorded only after native cleanup reports zero surviving process identities. Older journals without that proof remain held for inspection; they cannot be recovered merely because the old session is absent from the UI. Ambiguous multiple outstanding records for one employee also remain held.

Recovery writes a private pending receipt before publishing its terminal record, then a verified acknowledgement tied to the exact terminal-record digest after native validation. Startup keeps an adopted record held without that matching acknowledgement. A valid acknowledgement is explicit completion evidence even if a caller lost the response; it is not inferred from a filename or a completed phase alone. Receipts use bounded, no-follow reads, private modes and hashed move identifiers. Existing receipts are retained and conflicting acknowledgements fail closed.

The endpoint is `POST /api/plans/moves/:id/resolve` with `action: "adopt-existing"` and exact `oldSession`, `newSession`, and `toAccount`. It requires the same authenticated owner and same-origin boundary as other owner plan operations. Agent headers and limited logins do not authorize it. Every slow inspection is followed by a fresh owner/binding fence before the journal commit.

## What the office does by itself

Under the laptop runtime the office supervisor does not leave a held move to the owner. It continues a held laptop move up to three times through `continue-original`, with the supervisor's own requester for the original of that move, and then abandons it through the same service call the owner's `POST /api/plans/moves/:id/abandon` makes, with the supervisor's guard in place of the owner's sign-in. An error the office does not recognize, a missing continue step and a decision screen are handled the same way. A move that waits at the sign-in screen of a plan that is not signed in is not continued until the plan is signed in, and is abandoned after thirty minutes. The rules, the times and what the employee is left with are in [owner operations](owner-operations.md#a-plan-move-that-stopped-part-way).

The abandonment writes the journal only. It records in the closed move's sentence that the office closed it on the owner's standing rule (company setting `supervisorAutoRepair`) and what the move had stopped with. It takes no executor, so it cannot issue, repeat or inspect a provider launch; a paid dispatch on record stays exactly as it was, and a closed move can never be continued.

Abandoning does not move an employee back to the plan they came from once the move has switched their record to the new plan, which it does immediately before it launches the agent in the new laptop. Before that point the employee is still on the old plan and the laptop rebuild restores their laptop. After it they stay on the new plan and session, and the laptop's ordinary repairs apply.

A held move from before the laptop runtime is closed by the supervisor as well: by this document's `adopt-existing` when the employee already works on its destination session and the source cleanup is durably recorded, with the supervisor's executor and guard where the owner's request stands; otherwise by `PlanMoves.settleRetiredMove`, which closes the record as `failed` with its reason, releases the employee and changes no session, binding or receipt. It refuses a laptop move, a resource transaction, a move that is working and a caller whose guard no longer holds. The supervisor calls it only for an employee who works in a laptop.

New optional journal fields and recovery receipts belong to the new application schema. Do not insert them into an older running website's strict journal format or treat an old image as a rollback. Live repairs of legacy records require separate verified native evidence, an exact backup and explicit owner/coordinator review; this source change performs no such repair.
